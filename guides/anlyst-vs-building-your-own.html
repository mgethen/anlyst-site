Anlyst vs building your own stack

Anlyst Global Intelligence. Updated November 2026.

The short answer

A capable team can wire a general AI assistant, a few agents and a spreadsheet into something that drafts well on one deal. What is hard to build, and harder to keep running, is the part underneath: verification before anything enters the deal, a register of cited facts, versions, and a memory that outlives the person who built it. Anlyst is that part, set up in a week, and it works with the assistant you already use.

Why firms try it

The tools are good and they are cheap. Give a general assistant a set of deal files and a few agents and it will produce weeks of drafting in hours. The first result is impressive, and it is natural to think the rest is a matter of a few more prompts.

What you end up having to build

  • An intake that sorts and checks. Which documents are current, which are superseded, which cannot be read. Rules for units, scale and legal names, applied every time. In Anlyst this is The Gate.
  • A register of facts with their sources. Every figure tied to its document and page, with the arithmetic done in code and not by the model. In Anlyst this is The Register.
  • A report that keeps up. A question list that tracks its answers, and versions that show what changed when a new document arrives. In Anlyst this is Kickoff.
  • A memory that belongs to the firm. What was learned on the last deal, who knows whom, and what was said last time, available to the next person and the next mandate.
  • Confidentiality you can explain to a client. Each client's material kept apart, every model call logged, and an answer for the NDA that bars public AI processing.
  • A way to choose models. The best model for each job changes often. Anlyst handles this with The Route: the best model for each job, measured, with the cost known per document.

The cost that does not show at the start

A home built stack lives in the head of whoever built it. Each new model, each changed tool and each new kind of document is theirs to fix, in hours that would otherwise go to deals. When that person leaves, the firm is back where it started: teaching a brilliant intern the firm again.

When building your own is the right call

  • You have an engineer on staff and a narrow, repeatable task to automate.
  • You are still learning what these tools can do for your firm.
  • You want drafting help on one deal and do not need the firm to rely on the result.

You can keep what you have built

Anlyst works inside the assistant you already use. The prompts and habits a team has built for drafting keep their value: they now sit on verified facts with sources, and on a memory of the deal that does not depend on any one person.

Questions people ask

Can a small advisory firm build this itself with AI agents?

It can build the drafting. The hard part is underneath: checking what comes in, keeping every figure with its source, tracking questions and versions, and keeping it all running as models and tools change.

Do we lose the prompts and workflows we have already built?

No. Anlyst works inside the assistant you already use, so existing prompts and habits sit on top of the deal's verified facts.

Is Anlyst tied to one AI model?

No. The Route sends each job to the model that measures best for it, with the cost known per document.

How long does Anlyst take to set up?

A partner sets it up in a week, with the firm's own documents ingested and the Kickoff report in hand.
