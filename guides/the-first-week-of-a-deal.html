What the first week of a deal looks like

Anlyst Global Intelligence. Updated November 2026.

The short answer

In most advisory firms the first week of a mandate is spent moving numbers around: rekeying documents into Excel, reconciling formats and periods, and writing the first question list by hand. The first internal review meeting happens five to seven days in. With Anlyst, that meeting happens the same day the client's files arrive.

The first week today

Nobody can say what they think about a deal until an analyst has spent a week moving numbers around.

  • Rekeyed. Documents to Excel, by hand, for every deal. Audited accounts, management numbers, data room exports, all transcribed again.
  • Reconciled. Formats, periods and restatements fought into one view. A prior year that changed in a later set of accounts is found late, if at all.
  • Revisited. The same source documents reopened at every stage: the model, the IM, the IC paper, due diligence. Each pass starts from the PDF.

The partner's judgement, the firm's most valuable asset, waits at the end of the queue.

The first day with Anlyst

  1. The files arrive and The Gate sorts them. Each document is triaged: used, superseded, image only, unreadable or out of scope. Units and scale are read from the page, never assumed. The legal name comes from a registry extract and nothing else.
  2. The Register is built. Every figure is extracted and held with its document and page, one click from the original.
  3. The checks run. Consistency checks and anomaly flags. Ratios and cross foots are computed in code, never by the model.
  4. Kickoff lands. The report shows what the room holds and what it means, with the first question list and the question email to the client drafted.
  5. The team meets. The first internal review meeting happens the same day, and it starts from the partner's view of the business, not from a status update on the spreadsheet.

The rest of the week, and the rest of the deal

Answers come back from the client and the report is versioned, so the team sees what changed. From there the materials, the target lists, the data room and diligence are all drawn from the same verified facts, through to close. Ingest once. Every work product inherits the same verified facts, and the firm keeps everything it learns.

Questions people ask

How long does it take to get to a first internal review meeting with Anlyst?

The same day the client's files arrive, against five to seven days by hand. This is measured on live mandates.

What happens when a document cannot be read, or a figure has no stated unit?

The Gate flags it and raises a question. Nothing is assumed: a legal name, a currency or a scale comes from a source that states it.

What happens when two documents disagree?

Both figures are kept, each with its source, its period and its status. The difference is flagged and raised as a question. Anlyst does not choose one for you.

How long does it take to set Anlyst up?

A partner sets it up in a week, with the firm's own documents ingested and the Kickoff report in hand. No engineers in the room. That gets the firm to its first Kickoff report on a live mandate. Templates, question lists and the firm's memory build from there, deal by deal.

What is in the Kickoff report?

A financial summary, the checks and anomalies found, and the first question list, with the question email to the client drafted.
