How to test Anlyst on a real data room

Anlyst Global Intelligence. Updated November 2026.

The short answer

Do not judge a deal platform on a demonstration. Take a real, messy set of documents from a past mandate, thirty to fifty files in mixed formats, and run eight tests. Time it against your own team working by hand. The same tests work on Anlyst, on an enterprise platform and on a stack you have built yourself.

What to bring

Use a closed mandate where you already know the answers, handled as its NDA requires. Leave the mess in: scanned pages, superseded drafts, a spreadsheet with no stated scale, two documents that disagree. A tidy test set tells you nothing.

The eight tests

TestWhat to includeWhat a good answer looks like
1. Mixed documentsPDFs, scans, spreadsheets and superseded versionsEvery file is sorted: used, superseded, image only, unreadable or out of scope. Nothing is quietly dropped
2. Missing units and namesA table with no stated scale, and a trading name where the legal name is neededIt asks. It does not guess
3. Source tracingPick ten figures at random from the outputEach one opens its document and page
4. Conflicting figuresTwo documents that disagree on the same numberBoth are kept, the difference is flagged, and a question is raised
5. ArithmeticCheck the ratios and cross foots by handThey tie, because they were computed in code and not by the model
6. A revised documentAdd later accounts with a restated prior yearA new version of the report shows what changed
7. The next personSomeone who was not on the deal asks a question a week laterAnswered from the deal's facts, with sources, without uploading anything again
8. Confidentiality and exitAsk where documents go, what is logged, whether anything trains a shared model, and what you keep at the endPlain answers, and your own copy of the deal's facts

Measure the time

Record how long it takes from the files arriving to a first internal review meeting, and compare it with what the same mandate took by hand. In most firms that is five to seven days. Then check the accuracy: the measure that matters is analyst time saved without the numbers getting worse.

How Anlyst approaches each one

  • Tests 1 and 2: The Gate. Documents are triaged before anything is extracted. Units and scale are read from the page, never assumed, and a legal name comes from a registry extract and nothing else.
  • Tests 3 to 5: The Register. Every figure is held with its document and page. Consistency checks and anomaly flags run across the set, and ratios and cross foots are computed in code.
  • Test 6: Kickoff. The report is versioned, so a new document produces a new version and shows what changed.
  • Test 7: Memory. The deal remembers, so the firm does. The assistant you already use answers from the deal's facts and their sources.
  • Test 8: Trust by design and Archive. Each firm's data is walled off, every model call is logged, nothing trains a shared model, and at close the deal's facts are kept with the rest of the deal record.

Questions people ask

How should I evaluate an AI platform for M&A deal work?

Run it on a real, messy set of documents from a past mandate, not a demonstration. Check that files are triaged, that nothing is assumed, that every figure traces to its page, that conflicts are flagged, that arithmetic ties, that revisions show what changed, that a new person can pick the deal up, and that you keep your own copy of the facts.

What documents should I use for the test?

A closed mandate where you already know the answers, thirty to fifty files in mixed formats, handled as its NDA requires. Leave in the scans, the superseded drafts and the documents that disagree.

What happens when two documents disagree?

Both figures are kept, each with its source, its period and its status. The difference is flagged and raised as a question. Anlyst does not choose one for you.

What does verified mean?

It means every fact is traced to a source that states it, and anything missing or inconsistent is raised as a question. It does not mean the source is right. That judgement stays with the deal team.

What do we keep if we stop using Anlyst?

Your documents stay in your own folders, and materials are made in your own templates and file formats. At close, Archive stores an export of the deal's facts with the rest of the deal record, so the firm holds its own copy.

Do these tests only apply to Anlyst?

No. The same eight tests apply to an enterprise platform, a general AI assistant or a stack you have built yourself.
